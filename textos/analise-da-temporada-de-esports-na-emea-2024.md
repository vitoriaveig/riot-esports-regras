RECORDS

Our community is engaging with Riot esports more than ever,
and the numbers certainly show this. Our 2023 global event
viewership was incredible, with new formats at the Mid-Season
Invitational (MSI) and Worlds, combined with deeper co-strea-
ming integrations, driving 58% and 65% year-over-year growth
in Average Minute Audience (AMA), respectively. In aggregate,
regional LoL Esports leagues also saw AMA growth in 2023, up
16% year-over-year. The popularity of this event was reflected
when Riot Games earned its fifth Sports Emmy win for its bro-
adcast of the historic Worlds 2023 final, taking the award for
the second straight year.

This trend rallied further in 2024, with record-breaking viewership
numbers, for the start of multiple league properties. As the year
progressed, the League of Legends Mid-Season Invitational, held
in Chengdu, China, became the most watched edition of the event
to date, while VALORANT Masters in Madrid, Spain, earned its
place as the game’s most watched esports tournament so far. ⌫

RULES & COMPLIANCE



INTRODUCING:
EMEA ESPORTS
DISPUTE
RESOLUTION
Riot’s first internally-developed dis-
pute resolution mechanism for esports
launched in 2024, with initial aims to
strengthen the stability of the EMEA
ecosystem.

As expected for any form of professionalized competition, disputes
can arise between players, coaches, and teams, covering a broad
range of areas including salary obligations, prize money, and trans-
fer agreements. While Riot Games serves as the governing entity for
its esports competition circuits, there is a clear need for a neutral
arbitrator with expertise to resolve such matters and ensure a level
of integrity, professionalism and contractual stability remains within
esports competition.

The EMEA Esports Dispute Resolution (DR) is designed to tackle these
issues by providing a centralized, efficient, and accessible dispute
resolution mechanism. By focusing on fairness and transparency, the
Tribunal aims to resolve conflicts swiftly and equitably.


 ⌧⎇↓                                                              ↓


▶ The DR will address and resolve contractual disputes within the
EMEA region's League of Legends and VALORANT professional and
semi-professional ecosystems.

▶ It is designed to be a fair, efficient, and accessible platform
for resolving conflicts amongst players, coaches, teams and other
stakeholders.

▶ Disputes are addressed only with mutual consent, and procee-
dings are streamlined with a default rule of no hearings and only one
round of submissions.

▶ The DR will be operated by an independent body of Arbitrators,
that will oversee all decision making independent of Riot Games.


The DR is a pilot program for the EMEA competitive region. We will
offer more insights into its processes and rollout in the 2025 EMEA
Esports Season Review. ⌫

Having won Winter, Spring, and Summer, G2 Esports
effectively flipped our format; with three teams now
qualifying for Season Finals via championship points. This
was hardly the last upset in the script; in their first upper
bracket confrontation, G2 Esports fell against MAD Lions
KOI in a marathon series - game three of which saw the
most kills of any LEC game this year (64). GIANTX had
repeatedly qualified and lost their first split playoffs mat-
ches this year, and sadly did no better in Season Finals,
exiting early alongside SK Gaming with 0-3 eliminations.
       Team BDS started strong in their lower bracket match
against G2 Esports with a 0-2 lead. Just one more win
and they would have canceled the reigning champions
ticket to Munich, but G2 avoided an anti-climatic end
to their LEC season and reverse-swept the series, with
BDS only nabbing one kill in the final game. Despite a few
heartstopping losses in their upper bracket final, Fnatic
bested MAD Lions KOI and could enjoy a short respite
before the grand finals.
       The LEC semi-finals kicked off on the Olympia-
halle stage with an initial win for MAD Lions KOI. The
momentum was short lived, with G2 Esports gliding
through three games that each lasted barely half an hour.
This confirmed MAD Lions KOI as EMEA’s third seed to
the World Championship for a second year running. The
grand finals could not have had a more satisfying start:
the longest LEC game of the year (51:20) went down to
a chaotic wire, with Fnatic at one point losing their Nexus
towers to a minion wave. Although the proceeding three
games were unquestionably close, G2 Esports once again
emerged victorious as LEC champions for the second
row running, also becoming the first team in the league
to take the “Grand Slam” with their combined splits and
final victory. ⌫

in-game competition for our most talented teams. Based
