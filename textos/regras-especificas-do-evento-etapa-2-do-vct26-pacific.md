1.​   Introduction and Purpose
      1.1.​ Background.
      VALORANT Champions Tour LLC, a Delaware limited liability company (“League
      Entity”), along with its affiliates (collectively, “Riot”), organizes a series of regional,
      cross-regional, and international esports tournaments and related media events under the
      name VALORANT Champions Tour (the “VCT”) which feature competitive play (both
      live and online) of the video game VALORANT (the “Game”) by professional esports
      players and teams. The following are each a “VCT League” or “League”: (i) VCT
      Americas, (ii) VCT Pacific, and (iii) VCT EMEA. The League Entity has entered into
      written agreements with certain team-owning entities (each, a “Team Entity”) governing
      right to compete in the VCT and their respective Leagues (each such agreement, a “Team
      Participation Agreement”). Pursuant to the Team Participation Agreements and as more
      fully described therein, the League Entity has granted each Team Entity the right to field
      a team that will compete in the VCT and its respective League (“Team”).

      1.2.​ Purpose.
      The League Entity has created this VCT Pacific Stage 2 Ruleset (the “Event-Specific
      Ruleset”) to set forth certain policies, rules, and procedures that will apply to all Teams
      participating in the VCT Pacific Stage 2 event. This Event-Specific Ruleset is in addition
      to, and not in lieu of, the VALORANT Official Competition Ruleset (the “Competition
      Ruleset”) and the VCT26 Roster Construction Rules (the “RCR”).

2.2.7.​ Tiebreakers
        ●​ VCT and League events that include formats where ties may occur between
            Teams shall use the tie-breaker process specified by League Officials and/or
            the applicable Event-Specific Ruleset. If no tiebreaker process is specified,
            the following tie-breaker process shall be used to determine who advances.
        ●​ Tie-Breaker Process:
                ○​     Head-to-head Match score.
                ○​     Head-to-head map differential.
                ○​     Head-to-head round differential.
                ○​     Split/event map differential.
                ○​     Split/event round differential.
        ●​ The following “Tie-breaker General Rules” will be applied for 3+ Team
            tie-breakers:
                ○​     If any Team(s) can be clearly ranked based on a single applicable
                       tiebreaker criterion (e.g., head-to-head match score), those Team(s)
                       will confirm their standing and be removed from the tie.

<!-- página 10 -->
              ○​        For any remaining Teams that are still tied under the same
                        criterion, the tiebreaker process will be reapplied from the
                        beginning among the tied Teams.
                     A.​       Example: In a 4-way tie, if Team A (3–0) and Team D (0–3)
                               can be clearly ranked as first and fourth respectively while
                               Team B and Team C remain tied (1–1), Team A and Team D
                               confirm their standings and are removed from the tie. The
                               tiebreaker process is reapplied from the beginning between
                               Team B and Team C to determine their final placements.
       ●​ In the event that multiple ties occur simultaneously, each tie shall be resolved
          independently within its respective subgroup. For each subgroup, the
          tiebreaker process will be reapplied from the beginning, using the standard
          tiebreaker procedures outlined in the ‘Tie-Breaker Process’ above. The final
          placement of teams will preserve subgroup hierarchy: the highest-ranked
          team within a lower-ranked subgroup may not be placed above any team in a
          higher-ranked subgroup, regardless of subsequent tiebreaker outcomes within
          the subgroup.
       ●​ This process will continue until all teams are fully ranked.
       ●​ If a tie still remains after applying all procedures, a Bo1 match may be
          implemented at the discretion of League Officials. An alternative process may
          be implemented on a case-by-case basis.

2.4.​ Skirmish Rulesets


      ●​ In matches where teams are of equal seeding, a 1v1 Skirmish match will be
         played to determine the higher seeded team for map selection purposes. At
         their sole discretion, League Officials may choose to utilise Skirmish
         matches to determine seeding in any other official match.
      ●​ Each team will be required to submit a participating player for the Skirmish
         alongside starting rosters prior to each match. The participating individual
         must be part of the starting roster for the match or the coach registered in
         GCD.
      ●​ Skirmishes will be played from a designated location and at a specified time
         communicated by League officials.
      ●​ League officials will outline areas that content teams and other team
         personnel may be present during the execution of the Skirmish Match.
      ●​ Participating players will be given a small time window to set up gear and
         check settings. League officials will instruct players on time allocated and
         when the skirmish is starting. These areas may differ in configuration to
         “Match Areas” outlined in the VCT Ruleset.
      ●​ Map layout selection will be made at random by League officials.
      ●​ Winners of the skirmish are defined as the first participant to reach a score of
         5 rounds.
      ●​ League officials have the right at any point to impose gameplay restrictions
         including but not limited to the use of certain agents, weapons, tactics.

3.​   Roster Rules
      3.1.​ ​Starting Rosters
      For each match of VCT Pacific Stage 2, the Team must designate ﬁve (5) starters from
      the Team’s Active Roster (as defined in the VCT26 Roster Construction Rules), which
      shall constitute the Team’s “Starting Roster”. Rosters are considered public at the
      submission deadline.

<!-- página 15 -->
      Requests to modify a Starting Roster for a Team’s first match on any day must be
      submitted no later than 10:00pm local time, or an hour after the conclusion of the final
      game of the day, whichever is the latest, the day prior to the team’s match.


      3.2.​ Substitutions during Tournament Play
      For Matches involving more than one map (i.e., a best-of-three or best-of-five Match), a
      Team may replace their then-current starters with substitutes from the Team’s Active
      Roster (as defined in the VCT26 Roster Construction Rules) in between maps, provided
      that the Team informs the opposing Team and receives approval from the referees of such
      substitution no later than two (2) minutes after the conclusion of the previous game. In
      the event that a player disconnects during a map and is unable to return within the
      allocated pause time, the Team will be permitted to replace the player with a substitute
      from their Active Roster. Any substitutions must result in the Team having an eligible
      roster.
      Once a Team decides to use a substitution, the substitute player must immediately enter
      the Match Area and will be given seven (7) minutes to complete the setup process.
      In the event of an emergency substitution where a player is unable to compete, a coach
      may serve as a substitute as a last resort. If this results in a violation of the residency rule,
      an exception may be granted, subject to approval by the League Administrator (Pacific or
      any Competitive Operations representative).

6.​   Precautionary Notes

      6.1.​ ​Player Negligence
      Any pauses or delays (broadcast or operations) due to player negligence may be subject
      to penalties and may not be granted a tech pause. Player negligence includes, but is not
      limited to, activities such as alt-tabbing or pressing keys that black out their monitors,
      refusal/hesitancy to use venue-provided peripherals when players have not brought their
      own backup peripherals in cases of technical malfunctions, frequent hand warmer bursts,
      etc. League Officials will have full discretion to determine instances of player negligence.


      6.2.​ Venue entrance for non-staff members
      Non-staff members must obtain written authorization from League Officials at least seven
      (7) business days prior to entering Back of House (BOH) areas. In the absence of such
      written authorization, non-staff members are strictly prohibited from entering BOH areas.
      League Officials retain sole discretion to approve or deny requests for access. Any
      unauthorized entry by non-staff members may result in penalties for the associated
      players or teams.


      6.3.​ Indoor Smoking Regulations
      Indoor smoking is strictly prohibited at the venue, particularly due to the venue being a
      government-owned property. Any violation of this rule may jeopardize the entire event.
      As such, smoking indoors is prohibited.
      Any observed or suspected violation must be reported immediately to League Officials or
      designated onsite administrators. If a League Official or onsite administrator reasonably
      suspects a breach, the matter may be escalated for formal investigation in accordance
      with league procedures.

6.6 ​ Non-Native and True-Stretched Resolution Ban
Starting with Kickoff for the 2026 season we are updating our policy to ban the use of
non-native and true-stretched resolutions to ensure fairness, stability, and a better
experience for players and fans.
   ●​ Competitive Integrity: Non-native resolutions alter the intended game state and
      can create unintended visibility differences. Standardizing resolutions helps
      maintain a fair playing field.​

   ●​ Performance & Supportability: These resolutions operate outside what we can
      reliably support and have been linked to stability issues, making troubleshooting
      difficult for both players and event staff.​

   ●​ Broadcast Quality: True-stretched POVs distort replays and spectator footage,
      impacting the viewing experience.​

   ●​ Operational & Security Concerns: Achieving true-stretched requires manual,
      multi-step workarounds that slow event operations, creating security risks and
      delays during setup and maintenance.

<!-- página 18 -->
​     Players playing with non-native and true-stretched resolutions may be penalized or asked ​
​     to readjust their settings to approved resolution forms.


​     6.7 ​ Headset Regulations
      At all times while onstage, players and coaches must wear their headsets properly, with
      both ear cups securely covering both ears.

      Headsets must not be removed, loosened, shifted off the ear, or partially worn during live
      match play.

      Minor fit adjustments are permitted, provided the ear cup remains fully covering the ear
      at all times (e.g., adjusting front-to-back positioning without lifting the ear cup off the
      ear).

      Full repositioning of the headset (including lifting an ear cup) is only permitted with
      referee approval and not during active round play.



​     6.8 ​ Team Arrival & Check-In Policy
      Teams must arrive and complete check-in by the designated check-in time.
      Failure to meet the official check-in time may result in penalties. Lack of impact on
      match operations does not, on its own, prevent penalties from being applied.
      Exceptions may be granted at the League’s discretion in cases of circumstances beyond a
      team’s reasonable control, where supported by appropriate documentation (e.g., traffic
      accidents, public demonstrations).


​     6.9 ​ Seat Departure
      Players must remain seated during live match play. Limited exceptions may apply for
      brief celebrations or incidental moments; however, excessive or intentional departure
      from the seat may result in referee intervention and potential penalties.​
